Mission
Founded in 1976, the Spina Bifida and Hydrocephalus Association of Quebec (ASBHQ)'s mission is to improve the quality of life of its members and those whose lives are affected by spina bifida. This is our reason for being, and we are at your disposal!
Description
The ASBHQ is committed to playing a vital role with affected people and their families. Whether after the diagnosis of spina bifida is announced, during pregnancy, or when an adult with spina bifida questions the progress of their case, the ASBHQ becomes an important resource. We understand the anxiety, confusion and worry experienced by those affected and their families. We work with heart to provide them with all the information available so that they can make the choices that are necessary in their individual case.
Here is a general overview of the ASBHQ mandate.
Support and encourage research to determine the causes of spina bifida and enable the improvement of treatments and prevention techniques.
Disseminate information regarding spina bifida and hydrocephalus in its publication Le Spinnaker. Organize information and training sessions, ensure the production of documentation, promotional and awareness tours across Quebec.
See to the development of services that meet the needs of people with spina bifida and their families.
Provide a consultation and reference service.
Raise awareness among the population of Quebec about spina bifida and prevention measures.
Assume a leadership and cohesive role with regional associations to share ideas and experiences across Quebec.
Prepare and monitor the distribution of advertising for the various media, so that the ASBHQ message is standardized and in accordance with already established policies.
Play a political role with government authorities and other decision-making authorities, for any question concerning the integration of people with disabilities, particularly people with spina bifida and hydrocephalus.
Provide support to regional associations by offering them expertise and tools for planning, organization, promotion, etc.
The ASBHQ attaches great importance to consultation and partnership with other organizations in the community, public, or parapublic sectors, in order to better develop services that will meet the needs of its members. Its partners or life members are the Constance-Lethbridge Rehabilitation Center (CRCL), the Physical Impairment Rehabilitation Institute of Quebec (IRDPQ), the Chaudière-Appalaches Rehabilitation Center (CRDP-CA) and the Shriners Hospital for Children.
We actively collaborate with the following organizations on issues of common interest: education, integration, school adaptation, daycare integration, adapted transportation, equity, family policy, employment, etc. : The Office for Disabled Persons of Quebec (OPHQ), the Confederation of Organizations of Disabled Persons of Quebec (COPHAN), the Workforce Adaptation Committee (CAMO) for persons with disabilities and the Quebec Association of Postsecondary Disabled Students (AQEIPS).

At Head & Hands, we believe in creating a society where all young people can participate and be inspired by the endless possibilities available to them. We offer a range of programs and services adapted to young people aged 12 to 25, regardless of their origin or insurance status.
Medical services: Our clinic offers medical services to young people aged 12 to 25, with or without health insurance. For more information, contact us at clinic@headandhands.ca.

Youth 2000 (J2K): Our youth center located in Décarie is a space for adolescents aged 12 to 17 to relax, participate in activities and workshops. Contact jeunesse2000@headandhands.ca with any questions.
For any questions or to contact us, write to us at info@headandhands.ca or call us at (514) 481-0277. We are here to support you and provide you with the resources you need.
Head & Hands has been working to empower young people in Montreal for over 50 years. Our services, peer education and community initiatives aim to help young people make informed decisions about their physical and mental well-being.

The Canadian Psychoanalytic Society (CPS)!

Psychoanalysis in Canada
We practice an intensive form of psychotherapy that promotes personal development and the resolution of unsatisfactory or painful life patterns. Our members work closely with individuals to improve their mental health by meticulously examining personal and interpersonal experiences, the interaction between past and present, the connection between body and mind, and the dynamic between fantasy and reality.
Psychoanalytic process
The process requires establishing a relationship of trust with the analyst, in complete confidentiality. The sessions generally take place 3 to 4 times a week, thus encouraging the emergence of the analysand's dilemmas and traumas. The analyst's particular listening allows us to explore the experience of the analysand through his emotional reactions, his thoughts, his memories, his fantasies, his dreams, images and bodily sensations.
Training and members
Our members are psychoanalysts, members of the International Psychoanalytic Association (API).
